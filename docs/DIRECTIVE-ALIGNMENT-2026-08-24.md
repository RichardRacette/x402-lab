# Directive Alignment — 2026-08-24

Status: **governing quality-check addendum for Milestone 4.5D**

## Directive

> **Product #2 is unknown. Use Codex to improve the quality and speed of product discovery, not to manufacture confidence in another founder-invented SKU.**

The project remains aimed at **profitable repeat external purchases**. The Machine Demand Observatory is an internal instrument serving that objective, not a new business.

## Quality-check conclusion

The current direction is stronger than immediately implementing another product because:

- payment capability is already proven;
- previous product candidates failed for commercial rather than engineering reasons;
- current machine-commerce activity is noisy and concentrated;
- revealed purchasing behavior is more informative than endpoint count or founder intuition;
- the existing buyer/shopper stack can support market research later without forcing a new seller product;
- provider-neutral analysis can later ingest evidence beyond x402 if useful.

However, this direction has its own failure mode: **building a research platform instead of doing research.**

Therefore Milestone 4.5D is time- and utility-gated.

## Engineering budget

Tonight's first useful slice should target roughly **90–120 minutes of Codex execution**, not an open-ended observability platform build.

Priority order:

1. normalized provider-neutral types;
2. deterministic fixtures + tests;
3. metrics for breadth, repeat, economics and concentration;
4. free x402stats collection;
5. one readable report;
6. manual import path / opportunity-card scaffold;
7. only then optional deeper adapters;
8. once the Observatory passes, proceed with the approved Kiroshi Optics companion viewer.

If items 1–5 do not produce better product-discovery insight than manual browsing, stop. Do not rescue the Observatory with a database, dashboard, AI scoring, scraping framework, or more providers.

## Discovery capital

The project has access to approximately **$300 of discretionary experiment capital**.

This is useful strategic capacity, not a spending target and not standing authorization.

Default remains **$0 spend**.

Use capital only when a specific purchase materially reduces uncertainty around a candidate opportunity, for example:

- bounded transaction-level market data;
- a short upstream API/data trial;
- compute required for a falsification test;
- a small external service required to reproduce buyer behavior;
- a domain or minimal deployment expense only after a real commercial test justifies it.

Before spending, record:

1. the hypothesis being tested;
2. why free evidence is insufficient;
3. the maximum amount at risk;
4. what result would cause `CONTINUE` vs `REJECT`;
5. whether the spend creates a recurring obligation.

Do not use the experiment budget for decorative hardware, Kiroshi UI polish, broad subscriptions without a test, or infrastructure that merely makes the project feel more serious.

Any real-money x402 purchase still requires explicit owner approval at execution time.

## Market-scope guardrail

The project is searching for a **machine-economy opportunity**, not necessarily an x402-only opportunity.

x402 is currently the best observable laboratory because transaction/payment activity is public and x402-lab already has working infrastructure.

Do not infer that the best Product #2 must be:

- x402-native only;
- crypto-specific;
- recruiting-related;
- a micropayment;
- an API endpoint;
- derived from the most common current x402 category.

A future candidate may be distributed through x402, MCP, MPP, conventional API billing, agent marketplaces or multiple channels.

The Observatory's normalized data model should stay provider-neutral enough to ingest adjacent machine-market observations later, but **do not broaden tonight's implementation until the x402 baseline proves useful**.

## Research hierarchy

Prefer evidence in this order:

1. broad + repeat external purchasing;
2. repeat purchasing from a small but clearly independent buyer set;
3. high-value purchasing with rational buy-vs-build economics;
4. cross-seller procurement behavior;
5. strong adjacent-market spend where machine-commerce data are missing;
6. current customer pain;
7. founder intuition.

Raw transaction count alone ranks below all of these.

## Candidate-product gate

No Product #2 implementation until a candidate has written evidence for:

- observed or strongly analogous demand;
- independent buyer breadth and/or repeat behavior;
- concentration caveat;
- why a competent buyer purchases instead of reproduces;
- current free/paid substitutes;
- lawful/reliable supply path;
- plausible variable cost and contribution margin;
- an advantage x402-lab can credibly develop;
- a cheap falsification test.

No single opaque opportunity score is allowed.

## Kill rules

### Kill the Observatory if

- it is merely a prettier x402scan/x402stats leaderboard;
- source data cannot support meaningful breadth/repeat/concentration distinctions;
- manual browsing plus a spreadsheet is faster and equally informative;
- paid data complexity exceeds the value of the research;
- implementation expands faster than the quality of the questions it answers.

### Kill a product candidate if

- free substitutes erase the buy-vs-build advantage;
- demand is mostly one internal buyer or self-generated activity;
- the scarce input is unavailable or legally unusable;
- margins are poor at a price buyers rationally pay;
- our only advantage is 'uses x402';
- the cheapest falsification test fails.

## Tonight's decision standard

A successful Codex session does **not** require a Product #2.

The desired end state is:

- better visibility into real machine purchasing;
- fewer misleading headline metrics;
- 3–5 disciplined opportunity cards;
- at least one non-obvious falsification target worth researching next;
- or a documented conclusion that the Observatory itself is not worth further engineering.

## Kiroshi Optics

`KIROSHI-OPTICS.md` defines the now-approved internal companion viewer.

It remains subordinate to this directive:

> **Build better eyes only after the underlying evidence pipeline works.**

Once the minimal Observatory core passes its usefulness gate, Kiroshi Optics is authorized to proceed without another strategic approval step.

Kiroshi may make the work fun and faster. It may not become the justification for the work, a second data architecture, or a sink for experiment capital.
