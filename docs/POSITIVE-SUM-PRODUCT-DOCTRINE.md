# Positive-Sum Product Doctrine

Status: **governing product doctrine**

Adopted: **2026-08-29**

## North star

> **Build useful systems that make people’s lives better and become more valuable as they help more people. Own meaningful parts of those systems, use the wealth and reputation they create to secure the founder’s family future, and reinvest that security into increasingly ambitious work. Success should give other people reasons to want the project to succeed.**

This doctrine does not replace profit as an objective. It defines the preferred mechanism by which profit is earned.

## Positive-sum rule

Prefer products where:

```text
buyer gets more useful work / lower friction / better access / better decisions
                    +
seller earns more when that value recurs
                    =
                 aligned upside
```

The strongest product is one whose commercial success is difficult to separate from genuine customer value.

## Founder objective

Family security, ownership, reputation and wealth are legitimate goals. They do not need to be disguised as charity.

The project should seek compounding assets:

- reusable software
- owned IP
- trusted distribution
- recurring revenue
- durable customer relationships
- proprietary operational knowledge
- reputation earned through repeated usefulness

The goal is not sainthood. The goal is aligned capitalism.

## Alignment test

For every opportunity card, answer:

1. **What does the buyer concretely gain when we win?**
2. **What causes our revenue to increase?**
3. **Is that same mechanism also increasing buyer value?**
4. **Who bears the downside when the product fails or is wrong?**
5. **Are we creating value, or merely exploiting misunderstanding, lock-in, opacity or information asymmetry?**

A candidate is stronger when repeat purchase is evidence that the buyer repeatedly receives value, rather than evidence that the buyer is trapped.

## Allowed hard edges

Positive-sum does **not** mean timid.

The project may:

- compete aggressively
- negotiate hard
- retain ownership
- protect IP
- charge what the capability is worth
- reject bad-fit customers
- discontinue weak products
- take asymmetric founder risk
- pursue high margins where value supports them

## Red flags

Treat these as structural warnings:

- revenue depends on buyers misunderstanding the product
- switching friction is more important than utility
- hidden risk is transferred to users
- transaction volume rises while user outcomes worsen
- low price is used to mask weak value
- provenance or uncertainty is presented as certainty
- marketing claims exceed what the evidence can support
- product economics work mainly because harms or costs are externalized

These do not automatically prohibit a product, but they require explicit justification and mitigation before implementation.

## Provenance and truth discipline

The project has already adopted context provenance as a security invariant. The same principle applies commercially.

Distinguish:

- what is observed
- what is inferred
- what is claimed by a third party
- what has verified provenance
- what remains unknown

A fluent explanation is not evidence. A visual match is not provenance. Transaction count is not independent demand. MCP adoption is not payment demand.

When evidence is incomplete, the product should communicate uncertainty rather than manufacture confidence.

## Reputation flywheel

The desired long-term system is:

```text
usefulness
  ↓
trust
  ↓
reputation
  ↓
opportunity
  ↓
ownership + revenue
  ↓
family security + risk capacity
  ↓
more ambitious useful work
  ↓
more usefulness
```

Reputation should be accumulated, not spent.

The best outcome is that customers, collaborators and observers have a rational reason to say:

> **Good. I want this project to win because its success makes something I care about better.**

## Product-selection implication

Product #2 is still intentionally unselected.

The positive-sum doctrine does **not** lower the existing evidence bar. A noble-sounding idea with weak demand is still a weak product.

A candidate must satisfy both:

1. **commercial evidence** — recurring demand, buy-vs-build advantage, acceptable economics, lawful/reliable supply and falsification survival; and
2. **alignment evidence** — x402-lab’s upside increases primarily when the buyer receives genuine recurring value.

## Decision rule

Before meaningful engineering time, ask both:

> **Does this increase our ability to identify or falsify a genuinely profitable machine-commerce opportunity?**

and

> **If this succeeds at scale, will customers have a reason to be glad that we succeeded?**

If either answer is weak, do not build yet.
